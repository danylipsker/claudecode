SVG and DXF flat, STEP solids, or a script that builds native SOLIDWORKS parts and an assembly. Plus JSON and PNG.

Ten short chapters that build the whole construction from one idea — rolling — to a working pair of gears of any shape. Every figure is live and computed by the same engine as the workbench, so you can break each one and see what breaks.

The approach here follows the one popularised by Morphocular in “What Gear Shape Meshes With a Square?” (April 2024) — rolling centrodes first, then conjugate profiles as an envelope. The underlying theory is classical: Euler on the involute, and Willis’ law of gearing from 1841. The wording, the figures and every number on this page are this app’s own.

Three sandboxes, each isolating one mechanism so you can push it until it misbehaves.

Unique Gears

Pick a shape for gear 1, choose how many lobes its mate should have, and the workbench computes the mate that meshes with it — the centre distance that makes the pair close, the pitch curves that roll on each other, and the conjugate outline itself as an envelope.

Roll treats the outline as the pitch curve. Mesh puts teeth on that curve and generates the true conjugate profile, teeth and all.

Export writes SVG and DXF flat outlines, STEP solids extruded to a thickness you choose, JSON and PNG — all in millimetres. For SOLIDWORKS it writes a PowerShell script: .sldprt and .sldasm are proprietary binary formats that only SOLIDWORKS itself can write, so the script carries the geometry, starts SOLIDWORKS, saves a part per gear and builds the meshed assembly.

Everything runs in this page. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is fetched. Inspired by Morphocular’s gear videos; the mathematics is classical conjugate gearing.
