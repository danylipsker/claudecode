# Sources behind Hyper Water

The app grew from the film *The Strange Reason All Life Depends On One Molecule* (Sundown Science, YouTube,
October 2026, 2 h 3 min; chapters: the molecule that should not be liquid · two gases and a bend · the bond
that is barely a bond · the ice that floats · the ocean that remembers heat · the liquid that pulls salt apart ·
what the water has been doing · the sculptor inside every cell · older than the Sun · oceans in the dark · the
liquid no one can fully describe · the strange reason) and the thirteen sources its description lists. The pages
retell none of the film; they explain the same science in their own words. This file records what each source
establishes, so that pages cite them correctly. Numbers here were read from the sources on 2026-10-15.

## 1. NASA JPL — "Astronomers find largest, most distant reservoir of water" (22 July 2011)
Two teams found water vapour around the quasar **APM 08279+5255**, more than **12 billion light-years** away:
**140 trillion times** the water of Earth's oceans. The quasar's black hole is 20 billion solar masses. The
vapour spans hundreds of light-years, at about −53 °C, five times hotter and 10–100 times denser than gas in
the Milky Way, yet 300 trillion times thinner than Earth's air. The Milky Way holds about 4,000 times less
water vapour, most of its water being frozen. Bradford's team (JPL/Caltech) used Z-Spec on the Caltech
Submillimeter Observatory from 2008 and CARMA; Lis's team used the Plateau de Bure Interferometer (2010).
*Astrophysical Journal Letters*, 2011. → the-largest-reservoir, how-water-forms-in-space.

## 2. ESA Rosetta — "First measurements of comet's water ratio" (9 December 2014)
Rosetta's **ROSINA** mass spectrometer measured the deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio of water at comet
**67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko** (8 Aug–5 Sep 2014): **5.3 × 10⁻⁴**, more than three times Earth's oceans
(**1.56 × 10⁻⁴**). D/H is a diagnostic of where an object formed and how much comets and asteroids gave to
Earth's oceans; the result argued against Jupiter-family comets as the main source (103P/Hartley 2 had matched
Earth in 2011). Altwegg et al., *Science* 2015. → the-deuterium-clock, comets-asteroids-and-earths-water.

## 3. USGS Water Science School — "Water density"
Density of water by temperature: 0.99987 g/cm³ at 0 °C, **1.00000 at 4.0 °C** (exact value 0.9998395 at 4 °C),
0.99802 at 21 °C, 0.99318 at 37.8 °C, 0.95865 at 100 °C. Ice is about 90 % as dense as water (density falls
about 9 % on freezing); about 10 % of an iceberg shows. Water at 4 °C sinks below colder water, so lakes freeze
from the top; if ice sank, lakes would freeze from the bottom up and might never thaw. **Heavy-water ice is
10.6 % denser than ordinary water** and sinks in it. Dissolved salt raises density; hydrometers measure it.
→ why-ice-floats, the-density-maximum, lakes-freeze-from-the-top, isotopes-and-heavy-water.

## 4. CNRS — "Earth may always have been wet" (Piani et al., *Science*, 28 August 2020)
Enstatite chondrites, thought to be dry and formed in the inner Solar System like the material that made Earth,
hold enough hydrogen to have supplied **at least three times the water of Earth's oceans** (13 meteorites
measured, 11 pristine; heated for days to drive off terrestrial contamination). Their hydrogen and nitrogen
isotope ratios match Earth's mantle; the oceans' hydrogen isotopes fit a mixture about 95 % from such material.
Earth's water may have been built in from the start, with comets and other sources adding later. (Enstatite
chondrites are about 2 % of known meteorites.) → earth-may-always-have-been-wet, where-the-oceans-came-from.

## 5. Scientific American / Nature News — "Earth has water older than the Sun" (Cleeves et al., *Science*, 25 September 2014)
Modelling the young Solar System's disk, Ilsedore Cleeves and colleagues found the Sun's radiation could not
have produced today's deuterium-enriched water from scratch; **30–50 % of the water in Earth's oceans predates
the Sun** (formed 4.6 billion years ago), inherited as interstellar ice from the cloud that made it. Interstellar
clouds are richer in heavy water than the Solar System, from cosmic-ray chemistry at low temperature. If so, water
is a universal ingredient of planet formation. → water-older-than-the-sun, the-deuterium-clock.

## 6. Physics Today — "Fast x-ray scattering reveals water's two liquid phases" (19 November 2020; Kim et al., *Science* 370, 978)
Anders Nilsson's group (Stockholm) heated a film of high-density amorphous ice with an infrared pulse, making
liquid water at more than 2,500 atm in "no man's land" below the freezing point, and watched it with timed X-ray
pulses: within tens of nanoseconds a **high-density liquid**; after a few microseconds a distinct **low-density
liquid**; after tens of microseconds crystalline ice (water there freezes within microseconds however pure). The
phase boundary between the two liquids ends at a critical point at some hundreds of atmospheres. In 2018 the
leading simulation arguing against the transition was found to contain a coding error. → two-liquids-in-one,
supercooled-water-and-no-mans-land, polyamorphism-of-ice.

## 7. NASA JPL — "NASA missions provide new insights into 'ocean worlds'" (13 April 2017)
Cassini's deepest dive through Enceladus's plume (28 October 2015) found **molecular hydrogen** — about 1 % of
the plume, which is ~98 % water with CO₂, methane and ammonia — produced by hot water reacting with rock on the
seafloor (Waite et al., *Science*). Hydrogen plus CO₂ is food for methanogens, the chemistry at the root of
Earth's tree of life. Hubble saw a probable plume on Europa in 2016 at the place seen in 2014, rising about
100 km (Sparks et al., *ApJ Letters*), over a warm, cracked region Galileo had mapped. Life as we know it needs
liquid water, energy and C, H, N, O, P, S; Enceladus shows nearly all (P and S then unconfirmed; phosphates were
found in 2023). Europa Clipper was planned for the 2020s (launched 14 October 2024, arrival 2030). → europa,
enceladus, what-life-needs, lifes-start-in-water.

## 8. Fecko, Eaves, Loparo, Tokmakoff, Geissler — "Ultrafast hydrogen-bond dynamics in the infrared spectroscopy of water" (*Science* 301, 1698, 2003)
Femtosecond infrared spectroscopy of the O–H stretch of HOD in D₂O, with simulations: the stretch frequency
follows the electric field on the proton; at short times the hydrogen bond oscillates underdamped with a period
of **170 femtoseconds**; the network's collective rearrangement decorrelates in **1.2 picoseconds**. → bonds-that-flicker,
what-a-hydrogen-bond-is, seeing-the-bonds.

## 9. Philip Ball — "Water is an active matrix of life for cell and molecular biology" (*PNAS* 114, 13327, 2017)
Revisiting Szent-Györgyi's "matrix of life": water is neither a mystical life-giver nor a passive solvent but an
active participant — a structured liquid and a polar, protic, amphoteric reagent. Hydration shells with slowed
dynamics, the hydrophobic effect driving folding and assembly, water molecules as parts of enzyme mechanisms
and proton wires, water at interfaces and in crowded cells; our understanding of water as liquid and solvent is
still incomplete. → the-matrix-of-life, hydration-shells-and-proteins, proton-wires-and-enzymes,
what-remains-unknown.

## 10. Nature News — "Height limit predicted for tallest trees" (19 April 2004; Koch, Sillett, Jennings, Davis, *Nature* 428, 851)
Climbing the Humboldt redwoods, Koch's team found leaves at the top in permanent drought though the ground is
wet: gravity and friction in the vessels limit the height water can be lifted to about **122–130 m**. The tallest
measured tree was then **112.7 m** (Hyperion, found 2006, is 115.9 m). Top leaves are small and crowded, their
cells unable to swell; wind breaks the highest branches; the trees are about 2,000 years old. → the-height-limit-of-trees,
transpiration-pull, negative-pressure-in-the-xylem.

## 11. Hebrew University — "The sweet taste of heavy water" (6 May 2021; Ben Abu, Jungwirth, Niv et al., *Communications Biology*)
Heavy water, D₂O, tastes **sweet** to humans (not to mice), through the sweet receptor **T1R2/T1R3**: lactisole,
which blocks the receptor, removed the sweetness in people and in cell assays. Shown with human tasters wearing
nose clips, mouse tests, cell assays and molecular dynamics. D₂O is about 10 % denser than H₂O and freezes and
boils at slightly higher temperatures. → isotopes-and-heavy-water, quantum-effects-in-water.

## 12. Sci.News — "Scientists create new crystalline form of ice" (18 February 2021; Gasser et al. and Yamane et al., *Nature Communications* 12, 1128 and 1129)
**Ice XIX**, a second hydrogen-ordered form of ice VI, made by Thomas Loerting's group (Innsbruck) by cooling
slowly at about 2 GPa and confirmed by Ryo Yamane's group (Tokyo): neutron diffraction and calorimetry; on
warming it goes to ice XV above 107 K and to ice VI at 128 K. Ice VI is stable near 1 GPa; ice XV (2009) was its
first ordered form; ice XI (below 72 K) is fully ordered ice Ih. With ice XIX, 19 crystalline ices were known.
→ hydrogen-ordered-ices, high-pressure-ices, the-ice-rules.

## 13. L. J. Henderson — *The Fitness of the Environment* (Macmillan, 1913)
A Harvard physiologist's argument that the properties of water (and carbon dioxide and carbon) are uniquely fit
for life: its heat capacity, latent heats, expansion on freezing, solvent power and surface tension, each shown
to be extreme and each shown to matter for organisms and the planet — so that the environment is "fit" for life
as much as life is fit for the environment. The book is the ancestor of the "fine-tuning" and habitability
arguments, and of this app's closing branch. Public domain; a scan is on Wikimedia Commons.
→ henderson-and-the-fitness-of-the-environment, the-strange-reason, could-life-use-another-liquid.
