Linear Half Toner — workflow

1 · Source

Load a raster image or an SVG, and set the physical size of the finished carving. Every dimension in the app is real millimetres (or inches) on your material.

2 · Correct

Fix the artwork before it becomes a halftone: crop, rotate, levels, tone curve, denoise, sharpen. Paint a mask here to protect areas from cutting, or to restrict a tool to one region. Judge it in the Corrected view.

3 · Pattern

Each tool has its own pattern, angle and pitch. Width modulation is the one that matters for V-carving: dark areas become wide lines, wide lines become deep cuts. Keep max width at or below the pitch or neighbouring lines merge.

4 · Tool

For a V-bit of included angle A at depth d, the groove is w = 2·d·tan(A/2) wide; a tapered engraver with a flat tip adds T. The app inverts that to turn width into depth. Up to three tools can share one job, each taking its own band of the tonal range — a wide V for the darks, a fine one for detail.

5 · Export

  • SVG / DXF outlines — closed variable-width outlines. Apply a V-carve toolpath in VCarve, Aspire, Carveco or Fusion and the CAM derives depth from shape width. Recommended.
  • DXF polylines with width — centrelines carrying per-vertex start/end width.
  • G-code — GRBL, Mach3, LinuxCNC or Marlin, with optional arc fitting, laser power mode and bed tiling. Dry-run above the material first.
