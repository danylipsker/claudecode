## What changed
- **Face gears, completed and committed**: `py/gear_step/face_gear.py` builds the tooth surface as the literal envelope of the shaper pinion's involute, but computed per radial station in 2D (shapely union of a few hundred shaper positions, clipped to the tooth-height band and one pitch wedge) rather than as a 3D boolean sweep — a station's section is the loft-ready boundary, and ten stations lofted with a shared uniform spline parametrisation give a 6-face space tool patterned and cut from the blank in one N-ary boolean (~4 s for a 40-tooth gear). Litvin's undercut/pointing limits (L1/L2) come from the rack-equivalent section and set the auto ring; the built gear's measured top lands agree with the formula. Verified by the pinion meshing through the built gear at four phases (near-zero overlap) and colliding when turned half a pitch. Full write-up in `docs/gear-math.md` §17, including the construction bugs found along the way (a shaper rotated about the wrong axis; an undersized sweep range; unshared spline knots bloating the STEP to 96 MB; OCCT's B-spline volume being 10–35% low, worked around with a tessellation-based volume in `meshcheck.tessellated_volume`). C# UI: Face gear card, FACE GEAR section, derived rows, reset defaults, thumbnail, `--uismoke --facegear`. 5 new tests; suite at 102. PROGRESS.md entry added.
- **Double-enveloping worm, in progress, NOT committed as working**: two new untracked files, `py/gear_step/globoid_worm.py` and `py/gear_step/throated_wheel.py`.
  - `globoid_worm.py`: `GloboidWormParams` (throat pitch diameter, wheel teeth, starts, pressure angle, envelope teeth controlling wrap/length); Hindley's construction — the worm is generated by a straight-edged tool fixed to the wheel, so every axial section of the worm's thread is an explicit polygon (two straight flanks tangent to the wheel's base circle, tip/root arcs, all about the wheel centre) that only needs to be rotated as the worm turns. `build_globoid_worm_solid` builds the hourglass core (revolve) plus one smooth loft per thread start, fused and trimmed to length. This part works: it builds a valid, correctly-shaped hourglass worm (confirmed visually via a rendered thumbnail and by checking the tip/root radii at several axial stations against the closed-form hourglass profile). One real bug was caught and fixed here: the two flank lines' lean signs were swapped, which built an upside-down thread (wide at the tip, narrow at the root) — caught by checking the section's z-span at the tip vs the root, not by eye.
  - `throated_wheel.py`: generates the wheel FROM the built worm (the same idea as `face_gear.py`'s shaper, applied polar instead of planar) — for each transverse station of the wheel, slice the worm's thread analytically (closed-form, not a mesh slice) over the generating motion, union the slices, and loft through stations into a space tool. This is the unfinished part. The worm-vs-wheel mesh check (`meshcheck.interpenetration_volume`) is currently reporting ~1000 mm³ of overlap at every phase, including the "correct" phase — i.e. the wheel is wrong, not just imprecise. Root-caused (but not yet re-verified) to the thread sampler excluding the wrong side of the tip circle when a station's radial line passes inside it, which let bands extend past the thread's actual tip; a fix was written and is on disk but the mesh check was never rerun against it before the interruption.

## Decisions
- Kept the globoid worm's thread section fully analytic (closed-form line/circle intersections about the wheel centre) rather than sampling the cylindrical-worm code, because Hindley's construction makes every axial section an exact polygon — no envelope approximation needed for the worm itself, only for the wheel it cuts.
- Generating the wheel by slicing the worm analytically (vectorized numpy over sample angles) rather than tessellating the built worm solid and slicing triangles, on the expectation that it would be faster and exact; this added real complexity (the psi-window inversion, the tip-circle horn splitting) that is exactly where the current bug lives. If the analytic approach doesn't converge quickly next session, tessellating the already-built worm solid and slicing its mesh (numpy on triangles, the way the STL-volume probes this session did) is the fallback — slower per station but far simpler, and every other piece of this project's geometry is already checked against a tessellated volume as the arbiter.
- Face gear docs/PROGRESS numbers (STEP sizes, timings, top-land measurements) were filled in from actual measured probe output, not estimated, including two rounds of revision after later fixes changed the numbers (loft density, pcurve removal) — placeholders were tracked explicitly and grep-verified as fully replaced before committing.
