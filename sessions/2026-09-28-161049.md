## Summary
Built **Hyper Driving** (HYPER-DRIVING/), a standalone driving-theory study app for Israel, in Hebrew first and then English. It covers traffic law, all 401 official road signs, good driving practice, and technical knowledge of vehicles (components, maintenance, troubleshooting, handling faults) for cars, motorcycles, trucks and buses, with practice questions and a mock exam. Content is a data pack that the Ministry of Transport can update with signed bundles. Both languages are complete (33 chapters, 188 lessons, 798 questions, 12 troubleshooting trees each). `validate --release` has 0 errors and about 1,350 Hebrew and 700 English screens were checked in the browser. After Dany compared the signs with a reference poster, they were redrawn to match it. Everything was committed as b2606a1.

## What changed
- App shell and screens: a plain-JS PWA with no build step. RTL/LTR comes from CSS logical properties; hash routes cover learn/lesson/signs/vehicle/trouble/practice/quiz/glossary/updates/settings, plus an editor mode for authority staff (HYPER-DRIVING/index.html, js/app.js, js/views.js, js/editor.js, css/drive.css, sw.js, pwa.json)
- Content engine: the pack loader has language fallback and dated facts. Legal numbers exist only as `{{fact:id}}`, `{{fine:offence}}` and `{{points:offence}}`, each with a source, a confidence level and future-dated `changes`. The markup language covers terms, signs, figures, widgets, callouts and tables (js/content.js, js/markup.js)
- Signed updates: canonical-JSON SHA-256 per file, with an ECDSA P-256 signature on the manifest. The app verifies it with WebCrypto against `trustedKeys` and stores installs in IndexedDB. Tested end to end: tampered or unsigned bundles are refused, and a signed one installs and shows a diff (js/update.js, tools/keygen.js, tools/publish.js)
- Signs and warning lights are drawn from data (shape, colours, draw items, 156 pack glyphs), so the authority can change them as well (js/signs.js, js/glyphs.js, js/dash.js, content/il/signs.json, dash.json)
- Practice: Leitner boxes; a mock exam with the official rules taken from facts (30 questions, 4 mistakes, 40 minutes); smart and mistake sets; sign and warning-light drills in both directions; live widgets for stopping distance, following gap, speed energy, four-stroke cycle, blind spots and tyre codes (js/quiz.js, js/widgets.js)
- Content (content/il/): he/ and en/ lessons, questions and trees; 643-term glossary with official term, jargon and the other-language term; 236 facts and 84 offences; figures; maintenance schedule; notices; changelog
- English pass: British spelling, the Israeli "expressway" and "non-urban road", and regulation letter suffixes written as 39K/39P/39J. The glossary notes and its 163 legal references were translated. The troubleshooting group names were aligned with the Hebrew.

## Decisions
- **Standalone app, not built on HYPER-CORE.** HYPER-CORE is English/TeX-oriented and another session was editing it. Driving needs RTL-first UI, legal facts and signed updates.
- **Original text only.** The official Ministry question bank (data.gov.il) was used only to check coverage, never copied. The reference poster (lavibetnua.co.il) was used only for visual comparison and is gitignored (HYPER-DRIVING/86913_ykq6.png). Don't commit it.
- **Every legal number is a dated fact.** A change in law or fines is then a data edit with an effective date, not a text rewrite. That is what lets the Ministry push updates.
- **English is a parallel translation, not independent content.** It uses the same ids, option order, references and tree structure, and the validator enforces this so updates stay in step.
- **British English, with the Hebrew term beside technical words.** Learners in Israel hear the Hebrew and the slang (מצמד / "קלאץ׳"), so English pages keep them.
- **Official sign numbers keep their Hebrew suffix in English** (127פ = the lit version), because that is the official number.
- **Prohibition bars follow the poster:** red "\" for prohibitions and black "/" for end-of signs, which are grey. An agent had flipped the bars; that was reverted.
- **Stop sign 302 is a raised hand, not text.** Several colours were also changed to match the poster: lane-change and dead-end signs are blue, and 510 is a raised hand.
- **Writer subagents run on Sonnet.** Opus subagents hit the weekly limit twice.
- **Committed straight to main,** like the other Hyper apps.
