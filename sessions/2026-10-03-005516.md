## Summary
Built **Hyper Projections** (`HYPER-PROJECTIONS/`), the fifteenth Hyper app: every way the three-dimensional
world is drawn on a flat sheet — the orthographic, axonometric and oblique views of the drawing office, linear
perspective with one to three vanishing points, curvilinear perspective with four to six plus fisheye,
panoramic, cube and dome mappings, some 45 map projections of the globe (Ptolemy to Equal Earth, with Tissot's
indicatrix and polyhedral nets), the projections of the sky (planisphere, astrolabe, star charts, sun-path
diagrams, sundials, domes) and the practical uses of each. Every page carries the matrix or formula, a hand
construction with named tools played step by step and practised on a board that checks each stroke, a
simulation, calculators, examples, a quiz, applications, history and sources. 205 concepts, 600 formula
calculators, 154 simulations, 234 constructions, 1004 quiz questions, 362 worked examples. The strict
validator, the headless test of all 205 simulation runs, the engine test suites (309 + 88) and the browser
self-test over all 237 pages (203 with constructions) all pass clean.

## Decisions
- Built as a Hyper app on HYPER-CORE (the folder `HYPER-PROJECTIONS/` already existed) rather than a
  Curves-Workshop clone, so that constructions sit beside the concept map, calculators, simulations and
  practice; the construction kit was ported into the core so other apps can use it.
- Conventions fixed in the engine: y up, viewer along −z, points as columns on the right;
  `P.perspective(d)` is the textbook singular matrix (eye at origin, plane z = −d); `P.axonometric(α, β) =
  Rx(α)·Ry(−β)`; map options `{lon0, lat0, lat1, lat2, rotLat (tilt about the centre point; 90 = transverse),
  rotGamma (azimuthal only), P, A, B}`; sky angles in degrees, RA in degrees; rise/set hours are counted from
  the jd0 passed (pass local midnight to read local hours).
- Coastlines are hand-authored (coarse) because fetching Natural Earth would be a download needing Dany's
  approval; the lab says so. Star coordinates are rounded to ~0.1°.
- Content was written by 15 parallel Sonnet writers, one topic group each, against `AUTHORING.md` and the
  three reference pages; their engine reports were acted on at once: hidden-line threshold for edge-on
  faces, `k.dim {line:true}` dimension lines, `k.point` fourth argument, polygon edges as practice targets,
  `k.arcThrough`, `k.angle {cw}`, `P.depthOf` under perspective, map extents respecting cut-offs, the exact
  (ψ-linear) rhumb line, the Goode outline seam, `P.maps.conic / faceOf / robinson.table / goode.lobes`, and
  the azimuth sign (eqToHor was mirrored east–west; my test had encoded the same error — fixed, tests added).
- Two mistakes in my reference pages caught by writers and fixed: calculators using 90 where angle variables
  are radians (`pi/2`), the astrolabe plate's zenith on the wrong side of the pole, the Mercator table's 75°
  value (202.8), and an over-optimistic note on the four-centre ellipse (now gives its measured error).
- The sky labs show the place's standard time (no summer time); the lab text says so.
