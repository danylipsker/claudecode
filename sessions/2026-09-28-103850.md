## Summary
Dany asked for a Hyper app on Richard Feynman's lectures, in HYPER-FEYNMAN (a folder he had created), with deep
graphical explanations of every topic and a brief explanation of every math term. Built "the same way" as the
other Hyper apps — outline, reference page and sims, AUTHORING section, 16 parallel writer subagents, then
integration and checks — plus two engine features that now serve all twelve apps: a math-terms glossary
and a quantum/QED/fields physics module. Everything validates, simtests and passes the browser self-test.

## Decisions
- No quotations of Feynman anywhere: his books and lectures are copyrighted, so every page describes his ideas
  in original words and cites the lecture, volume and chapter instead; the home page states that the app is an
  independent companion and links to Caltech's free online edition. A final sweep found only titles in quotes.
- The glossary was built into HYPER-CORE for all apps, not only Feynman, because the formula chips and the Math
  terms tab are useful everywhere. It grew from 93 to 113 terms as writers reported gaps.
- `stationaryPath` was rewritten from relaxation to Newton's method: relaxation was slow and diverged when the
  true path is a saddle of the action (a spring over more than half a period).
- A writer reported `Q.rng` as biased; a 20 000-player test showed it is not (spread within 0.1 %, and the
  72.9 % inside ±1σ matches the exact binomial value) — no change.
- The QED book is based on the Mautner lectures at UCLA; the 1979 Auckland lectures were an earlier filmed
  version — the home page wording was corrected accordingly.
- The Challenger O-ring sim uses an illustrative rubber-recovery model, labelled as such on the page.
