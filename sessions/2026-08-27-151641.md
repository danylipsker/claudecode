## Decisions

- **Ghosts are appended AFTER the translucent sheet slab, dead last in the model group.**
  WPF 3D blends a transparent triangle only against geometry rendered earlier, and the
  blades intentionally pass through the sheet's z-band (blades z 0–60, sheet mid z 30).
  In their natural draw position the ghost blades would punch blade-shaped holes in the
  sheet from the usual top-down camera. The accepted tradeoff: a ghost plate directly
  under the sheet is not visible through it (it vanishes rather than showing faintly) —
  harmless here, and arguably the point of hiding them.
- **Fading covers the whole tool — plate, label, and blade — not just the plate.** A solid
  black blade floating on a faded plate would defeat the purpose; the blade is the part
  that actually pokes through the sheet band in 3D.
- **One shared constant (`WorkboardRenderer.FadedToolOpacity`)** drives both 2D fade sites
  so the two renderings can never drift apart.
- **`_activeToolIndex == 0` (no selection) renders bit-identically to the old code** in
  both views — verified against a baseline, so nothing changes for users who never pick a
  tool.
- Verification reused the off-screen-MainWindow driver pattern from the curved-edges
  session; one test-side fix was needed (Helix's `MaterialHelper.CreateMaterial` returns a
  `MaterialGroup`, not a bare `DiffuseMaterial` — the app code was right, the assertion
  was unwrapping the material wrong).
