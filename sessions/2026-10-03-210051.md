## Summary
Fixed a geometry bug in the FASTENERS generator: the drill-point cone of the hexagon-socket and hexalobular
drive recesses was built at z = 0 instead of under the socket floor, which left an enclosed cone-shaped void in
the shank just under the head of cap, low-head, button, pan and shoulder screws, and a cone ring at the socket
mouth of countersunk and set screws. The cone now sits where it belongs, a self-test check guards against any
enclosed void, the DIN 94 split pin's own small pocket is gone, and 3,008 STEP files in 26 families were
regenerated with their previews, index, JSON and HTML. Afterwards the regenerated parts were checked in
SOLIDWORKS 2020 and every stale `.SLDPRT` under `FASTENERS\custom` was reconverted. Two other sessions (mate
references, spring washers / O-rings) were working in the same checkout the whole time.

## Decisions
- **Cone clipped by the walls, not a plain cone under the floor.** The literal fix (prism + cone of base
  radius e/2 moved to the floor) is valid but leaves six undercut ledges, because the cone's base circle is
  wider than the hexagon's flats. Intersecting a deeper prism with the cone keeps the original angle and
  radius and has no undercut. An inscribed cone (radius s/2) was rejected: its base circle is tangent to the
  flats, which is degenerate topology.
- **Flat floor for the pin-in-socket drives.** `_pin_tool` subtracts the pin from the tool; with a cone under
  the floor the pin would either come loose or leave another void under it.
- **Guard values chosen so the catalog is untouched.** A wall of d/4 would have shortened the cone on the
  shortest M10–M20 cup-point set screws for no real reason; 0.15 d clears every catalog size.
- **The task's premise about mass was only partly right.** It said masses were about 1 % low. A correctly
  placed cone removes nearly the same volume, so cap-screw families moved by +0.01 … +0.25 %; the real changes
  are +0.3 … +3.1 % for the pin-security variants (void gone, flat floor) and −0.3 … −4.1 % for countersunk and
  set screws (the cone is now really cut below the socket). DIN 94: +0.1 … +0.65 %.
- **DIN 94 fixed rather than exempted.** Asked whether to fix or exempt it in the new check, Dany answered "do
  all the problematic fasteners". Cause: OCC's torus with major radius < minor radius leaves its inner "lemon"
  unfilled, and the leg gap crossing it became a closed pocket. A scan of all 7,932 parts found no other.
- **Writing into the real checkout from a worktree session.** The Edit hook blocks the base checkout,
  `change_directory` is refused for a worktree session, and a granted `request_directory` does not lift the
  hook. Developed and verified in a copy inside the worktree, asked Dany, then wrote through the shell with a
  3-way `git merge-file` on top of the other session's edits.
- **Three sessions, one checkout.** Agreed by message who writes shared files and who runs the one
  `build_catalog.py` ("building now" / "build done"). Once a "Dany asked me to commit" message crossed my
  write: I put the other session's versions back at once and merged again after its push. My own commit was
  staged by exact path list behind a count guard, so the other session's `SwImport.cs` stayed out of it.
- **Demo folder rebuilt with its own tools.** Overwriting the four stale socket parts alone would have left
  the mates of `MateReferenceDemo.SLDASM` and `PlaceOnHolesTest.SLDASM` dangling.
- **"Stale" = older than the STEP `Catalog.GetStep` would pick.** That gave 30 files, not only the socket
  ones; parts from 15 September predating that day's rebuild were included.
- **Backup folder sent to the Recycle Bin**, not hard-deleted, when Dany asked to delete it.
