## Summary

Ported the CURVES.html vector exporters into VectorLab, which gained EPS, AI, WMF, EMF and
HPGL alongside its existing SVG, PDF, DXF and JSON. The port was a rewrite rather than a
copy: CURVES wrote plain black polylines, VectorLab has fills, dashes, holes and real
Béziers, so the two PostScript-family writers were rebuilt on the `pdfText()` model and keep
curves and paint. Verification through GDI+ and by reading the generated PostScript caught
two real bugs that structural checks would have passed.

## What changed

All in `VECTOR-EDITOR/vector-editor.html` (+321 lines, purely additive), committed as
`ee23e36`.

- `exportPaths(flatten)` extracts every visible shape once as page-space subpaths carrying
  the paint that applies (fill, stroke, width scaled by the shape matrix, dash, even-odd).
  `exportPolys()` is the flattened view the polyline formats consume.
- **EPS** — real `curveto`, per-shape colours, dashes, even-odd fill, correct EPSF headers
  and bounding box.
- **AI** — Illustrator 3.2 dialect, cubics kept, colours as CMYK.
- **WMF / EMF** — per-shape pen and brush, `Polygon` for closed filled paths and `Polyline`
  otherwise, `PS_NULL` pen when a shape has no stroke. Byte writers (`BW`, `concatBytes`)
  ported from CURVES.
- **HPGL** — `PU`/`PD` outlines with one pen per distinct colour, y flipped for the plotter.
- Five buttons in the Document panel, each titled with what the format keeps or loses;
  `exportVector()` dispatches through `VEC_EXPORTS` and reports the format's tradeoff in the
  status bar.

## Decisions

- **Rebuilt EPS and AI rather than porting them.** CURVES' versions stroked black polylines,
  which would have thrown away every fill, colour and curve in a VectorLab drawing. They now
  follow the same walk `pdfText()` uses (`contours` → `segKind` → `segCtrl`/`arcToCubics`),
  so all four PostScript-family and PDF exports share one geometry story.
- **Only WMF, EMF and HPGL flatten**, because their record sets have no cubic primitive —
  a format limit, not a shortcut. They still carry colour and fill, so they are not merely
  the CURVES output with better geometry.
- **AI uses CMYK (`k`/`K`)**, understood by every Illustrator version including 3.0, rather
  than a newer RGB dialect that might not load. The cost is recorded under Open threads.
- **Verification method — two bugs found, both invisible to structural checks:**
  1. `EMR_CREATEPEN` carried an extra field after `ihPen`, shifting the whole LogPen struct
     four bytes, so style, width and colour were misread and every stroke rendered hairline
     black — including shapes with no stroke at all. Found by rendering the EMF through
     **GDI+**, the engine Office itself uses. Header/size/record-count assertions all passed
     while this was broken.
  2. EPS filled every shape in its *stroke* colour: PostScript has one current colour
     (unlike PDF's separate registers), so setting fill then stroke up front left the stroke
     colour live when `fill` ran. Colour is now set immediately before each paint, with
     `gsave … grestore` between. Found by reading the generated PostScript, not by testing.
  **Structural validity is not correctness — render it or read it.**
- **Test scaffolding pattern, reused from the CURVES session and now proven twice:** slice
  the shipped writers out of the HTML by marker comments, `new Function(...)` them in Node
  with a small stub (here `app.doc` and `exportPaths`), and write real files to disk. The
  shipped code is what gets tested. `--dump-dom` is broken in this Edge build and the
  preview pane's `data:` origin cannot reach a loopback server, so this is the only route
  that works for getting bytes out.

## Open threads

- **DXF, EPS, AI and HPGL have still never been opened by their real consumers.** WMF and
  EMF are now GDI+-verified and SVG/PDF render in a browser, but no CAD tool, PostScript
  interpreter or plotter exists on this machine. First import into AutoCAD/QCAD (DXF),
  Illustrator (AI/EPS) or a cutter (HPGL) is worth a minute each; the writers are simple
  enough that a failure will be obvious.
- **AI cannot express even-odd fill.** The Illustrator 3 dialect has no even-odd operator,
  so a shape whose holes depend on it may fill solid. EPS, PDF and SVG are exact. If this
  matters, the fix is emitting a newer AI dialect with `XR`, or reversing hole winding on
  export so nonzero produces the same result.
- WMF is 16-bit, so the page is scaled to ~20000 units; precision is bounded but invisible
  at that scale. Text is exported as outlines via `flatShapes()`, same as PDF and DXF.
- The five buttons live in the Document panel, which ships **parked** — they are invisible
  until the panel is unparked. Fine, but worth knowing before concluding the port did
  nothing.
- Not ported from CURVES: nothing. Its SVG/PDF/DXF writers were deliberately left alone
  because VectorLab's own are better (styles, arcs kept exact).
