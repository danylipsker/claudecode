## Summary

Dany, a sliding block puzzle fan since childhood, asked for a sliding block app with the best GUI
and UX possible, at least 1,000 puzzles in order of difficulty, free choice of puzzle plus an
automatic jump to the next unsolved one, in the graphics and colours of the Sokoban page. Four
styles were named: arranging blocks in order (Towers of Hanoi, puzzleworld IPP19), Rush Hour,
Nick Baxter's classic and modern designs, and Klotski. The result is `SLIDING-BLOCKS/`: 1,250
generated puzzles in four kinds and ten stages, every par proven by a solver. It is committed,
pushed and live at https://danylipsker.github.io/claudecode/SLIDING-BLOCKS/index.html.

## What changed

- One engine for every kind: a one-line puzzle format (`family|name|board|goal|par|options`),
  goals as "piece at a place" or a pattern of classes, gates, piece classes, labels, and Panex
  depth zones; a breadth-first solver that merges alike pieces into one state
  (SLIDING-BLOCKS/sb-core.js)
- The page: Sokoban tokens and wall/floor tiles, pieces drawn as SVG polyomino outlines (wood,
  the teal hero with eyes, cars and trucks, colour classes, Hanoi-width disks), dragging that
  follows the pointer and turns corners, Tab + arrow keys, undo/redo, hints and Shift+H solve from
  the in-page solver, stars against par, win card with the exit animation, how-to card per kind,
  library with kind chips, ten stage buttons with progress, lazy thumbnails, jump / Random / Next
  Unsolved, saved progress, sound (SLIDING-BLOCKS/index.html, sb-app.js)
- The generator: random samplers per kind that keep the position farthest from the goal in its
  reachable set, Gridlock "evolve" by single-vehicle mutations, colour sort built backwards from a
  tidy goal, mirror-symmetric colour swaps, stepped-well and Panex towers; parallel sampling on all
  cores into a git-ignored cache, then a par ramp per kind dealt into one list
  (SLIDING-BLOCKS/tools/families.js, generate.js)
- The checker: re-solves all 1,250 from their starts on all cores and confirms each par
  (SLIDING-BLOCKS/tools/check.js); icon tool drawing icon.svg and the PNGs from one shape list
  (tools/make-icons.js); service worker `sliding-blocks-v1`, manifest, README
- Apps page card next to Sokoban (index.html)

## Decisions

- A move is one piece moved any distance, around corners too, and moving the same piece again
  straight after is still that move. This is the classic count: the solver gives L'Âne Rouge
  exactly 81 moves and 25,955 positions, the published figures, which validated it.
- Every puzzle is generated, not copied: puzzleworld's layouts are Java applets and Rush Hour cards
  are ThinkFun's. Only public-domain classics the solver confirms are named: L'Âne Rouge (81) and
  the farthest 8-puzzle position (31). The Pennant Puzzle (59) was left out because its layout
  could not be recalled reliably (the recalled layouts solved in 17 or not at all).
- The Rush Hour style is called Gridlock (trademark).
- Hints run the BFS in the page, so no puzzle may have more than 250,000 reachable positions
  (MAX_SIZE in generate.js); that is why there is no 4x4 fifteen puzzle and no four-disk Panex
  swap (1.09 M positions). The hardest hint measured takes about 0.2 s.
- Par ramps are per kind with a ceiling (Klotski 150, Release 120, Order 70) and a bend toward
  the easier, after the first build put half the Release puzzles above 87 moves. Two one-move
  teaching puzzles open each kind. Kinds are dealt together by their place on their own ramp, so
  every stage holds all four (34/33/30/28 per stage).
- Duplicates: two draws that reach the same set of positions are the same puzzle (or its mirror);
  the build drops matches on kind, reachable-set size and par (numbered trays and towers exempt).
- Towers: stepped funnel wells (a bar goes only as deep as it is narrow) gave at most 13 moves,
  so the Panex rule was added to the engine (option `z:`, disk k at most k deep). Under depths,
  pieces of different levels must never be merged as alike: the checker caught 6 two-colour
  towers with wrong pars from exactly that, and the grouping key now includes the level.
- Progress is keyed by a hash of the board and goal, not the puzzle number, so it survives a
  rebuild that reorders the library. A solve with hints scores one star and never masks a later
  clean solve.
- Committed straight to main, as this repo does, since Pages serves main. Pages did not start a
  deployment by itself after the push; a `POST repos/.../pages/builds` started it.

## Open threads

- Dany has not played it yet: difficulty feel, drag feel on a phone and the stage balance are
  untested by a person. Stage 10 averages 88 moves, dominated by long Klotski/Release puzzles.
- Only 32 Panex towers and 8 stepped-well towers; more Hanoi-style variety (four columns, deeper
  single towers within the 250k cap) could be added through towerSpread/panexPuzzles.
- Colour swaps (IPP19 style) are rare: the swap sampler succeeds on few boards (45 in the build).
- Regenerating needs `node tools/generate.js [minutes]`; the 8 MB candidate cache is local only
  (SLIDING-BLOCKS/tools/cache, git-ignored), and puzzle order changes on a rebuild (progress is
  safe, puzzle numbers in bookmarks are not).
