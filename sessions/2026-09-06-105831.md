## Decisions

- **Arc physics = the machine's own geometry.** Cutting an arc against the fixed blade
  pins the contact at (edgeX, toolY) and rotates the sheet with the arc tangent; the arc
  centre is then STATIONARY in machine space and the pincher orbits it. So the dialect
  extension is honest standard G-code: `G2/G3 A B C R F` — end pose in joint angles, R =
  the pincher's orbit radius (negative >180°, standard R-format), C interpolating linearly
  like any rotary word. G2 = clockwise, G3 = counterclockwise, per Dany's spec.
- **Safety reuses the existing candidate machinery**: arc validity probes the straight-side
  tests at the run's first/middle/last sub-sides and requires ONE (orientation, edge)
  choice to cover the sweep; otherwise automatic fallback to tessellated cutting with a
  program comment. No new validity math was invented.
- **Retract = reverse sweep** (opposite G2↔G3 at F1800) back through the curved kerf —
  consistent with the straight cycle's back-on-track philosophy.
- **Arcs only for now**: explicit Arc edges + circular corner fillets become G2/G3;
  Beziers/splines/NURBS still cut tessellated (a future biarc-fitting pass could convert
  them). Full-circle runs split into two half-arcs (R-format can't express 360°).
- **Tools Table follows house patterns**: CSVs next to the exe (six files in
  `tools_table\`), modal editor window, seed-on-first-run, selection stored as catalog
  state (`selection.csv`) rather than in machine_parameters.
- **TPI is a string** in the catalog — progressive blades ("17-24") are real.
