## Summary
Built **Hyper Optics** (`HYPER-OPTICS/`), the sixteenth Hyper app, from Dany's request for "a very
comprehensive optic study app" in which people "start understanding terms". It covers rays, reflection,
refraction and Snell's law; lenses, stops, the f-number, aberrations and the doublets and triplets that
correct them; interference, diffraction, gratings and polarization; glasses, coatings, filters and
components; cameras, CCD and CMOS sensors, the C, CS, F and other mounts, field of view, focus, optical and
digital zoom, shutters and MTF; lamps, bulbs, bases, ferrules, illumination and fibres; lasers, their
families, Gaussian beams and beam shaping; the eye, its deficiencies, the optometrist's methods, spectacles
and multifocal lenses; colour and Munsell; instruments, scanners and metrology; illusions and phenomena; and
a last branch that reads whole optical systems part by part. 503 concepts in 11 branches and 42 topics,
1,562 formula calculators, 478 simulations (529 uses), 2,515 quiz questions, 975 worked examples, eight labs
and a dictionary of 2,848 terms. The strict validator, the engine tests (499 + 200), the lab test (61 pages),
the headless test of every simulation (578 runs) and the browser self-test over all 556 pages pass clean.

## What changed
- **A new concept field, `terms: [{ term, also, def }]`** (`hyper.js`, `ui/concept.js`, `ui/app.js`,
  `validate.js`). Every page shows a "Terms on this page" panel; all terms together make Tools → Optics
  dictionary and are in the search box. This is the answer to "start understanding terms": a reader can
  begin from a word (AOI in its three meanings, OD, MTF, NA, EFL …) and land on the page that teaches it.
- **The optics engine** on `Hyper.optics` / `kit.optics`, three files in `HYPER-CORE/js/`:
  `optics.js` (about 45 materials with dispersion, Snell, Fresnel, thin lenses, ray matrices, an exact 3-D
  ray tracer for lens prescriptions with pupils, real-ray aiming, spot diagrams and Seidel sums, designs
  and a lens library from singlet to double Gauss and the eye), `optics-wave.js` (thin-film stacks and
  coating designs, diffraction and gratings, MTF, Gaussian beams, laser cavities and safety, Jones, Stokes
  and Mueller polarization), `optics-vision.js` (CIE colour from the tabulated 1931 functions, CIELAB,
  Munsell, colour-vision deficiency, photometry and source spectra, lamps, bases and bulbs, cameras,
  sensors and mounts, the eye and spectacles, scanning, beam shaping, fibres).
- **A drawing kit** `opticsym.js` (`kit.osym`: lenses, mirrors, rays, stops, sensors, prisms, beams,
  spectra, fringes, whole systems from a prescription).
- **Eight labs and the dictionary** (`ui/optics-*.js`, `ui/optictools.js`, wired in `ui/views.js`): ray
  bench, lens lab, camera and lens, coatings and glass, colour lab with the Munsell wheel, eye and glasses,
  lasers and beams, illusions (27 figures).
- **The content**: `HYPER-OPTICS/content/` (42 topic files, the outline, three reference pages) and
  `HYPER-OPTICS/sims/` (43 files), plus `AUTHORING.md` (the writers' guide with the engine reference) and
  `README.md`.
- **Tools**: `tools/test-optics.js` (499 checks of the engine), `tools/labtest.js` (a headless run of every
  lab page). `validate.js` and `simtest.js` now accept a bare topic name after `--only`; before, a bare name
  matched no file and both tools still printed OK.
- `units.js` gained photometric and laser quantities and units (luminance, efficacy, spatial frequency,
  fluence, radiance, kW/cm² … GW/cm²); `simkit.js` exposes the two kits; `hyper.css` styles the terms panel,
  dictionary and labs.
- The other fifteen apps' `index.html` load the Optics catalog for cross-links; the landing page and the
  root `README.md` list the app.

## Decisions
- **Terms first.** The dictionary and the per-page terms panel were added to the engine instead of a
  glossary page, so that the definitions live with the page that teaches them and cannot drift apart.
- **Numbers come from the engine.** Writers were told to take every quoted number from `kit.optics` or a
  checked calculation. The cost: a behaviour-changing fix to the engine makes page text stale. Three audit
  passes re-measured pages after fixes to the ray tracer (real-ray aiming at the stop, the intersection root
  far from the vertex, the achromat trimmed for thickness). After that the ray tracer and the tables were
  treated as **frozen**: later reports of approximations were answered with local models in the simulation
  files, not engine changes. Safe additions (a deuterium spectrum, table corrections) were still made.
- **Engine data corrected on writers' reports**: G13 base 12.7 mm (was 13); the single-mode fibre entry is
  an 8.2 µm core with NA 0.12 (9 µm is the mode-field diameter; the old entry was not single-mode at
  1310 nm by the engine's own formula); the ends of the spectral locus are tied to the published points;
  `acuityFromDefocus` is 20·(1+D)²; confocal cavity waist has its closed form.
- **Left as approximations on purpose** (changing them would move numbers already on pages): white-LED
  spectra are one broad Gaussian, the scotopic curve peaks at 503 nm, "daylight" is a 6500 K Planck curve
  and not D65, `fibre.modes` is the large-V formula, laser-safety numbers cover visible continuous light
  only, the progressive-lens model has a narrow corridor, the silicon index is the infrared value.
- **Reported engine "faults" that are real behaviour**: a flat-side-first singlet loses its rim rays for a
  near object (they miss the second face); `fresnelFacet` is for parallel light entering the flat side (the
  comment was corrected; the lighthouse page computes the other arrangement itself).
- **Health pages** (visual deficiencies, optometry, spectacles) explain how things work, say that they do
  not diagnose, carry "seek urgent eye care" notes where sight is at risk, and give no treatment advice.
- **One writer per topic, in parallel** (Sonnet subagents, about fifteen at a time, each writing exactly
  one content file and one simulations file from a shared brief). Dany stopped the coatings writer by
  mistake at 9 of 12 pages; it was left stopped and the three missing pages (neutral density, how coatings
  are made, laser damage) were written in the main session.
- **No brand names** in the system pages; kinds of product only.

## Open threads
- **Sources are unchecked against the publications.** Citations, history dates, prevalence figures and
  typical values (damage thresholds, lamp efficacies, clinical ranges) come from the writers' knowledge.
  Those spot-checked matched; none was looked up. The README says so.
- **Most simulations were never seen by eye.** All 478 run headless and mount in the browser, but only
  about a dozen pages were looked at. Writers could not view their drawings. Known: a few labels overlap
  on phone-width stages (seen on telecentric-imaging: "stop" over "sensor"; reported for machine vision,
  laser principles, fibre optics below about 420–480 px).
- **The coating-chamber animation** (`cf-deposition`) was seen drawing and starting, not growing a layer
  at full speed, because the browser pane throttled animation while in the background.
- **Laser damage thresholds** in `cf-damage` are typical orders of magnitude from memory, labelled so.
- **Fata Morgana** mode of the mirage simulation gives one upright and one inverted image, not a stack.
- **Engine gaps the writers worked around locally** and that could move into the engine if a second page
  needs them: a polygon/prism ray tracer, CIEDE2000, a partial polarizer and rotator in `O.pol`, Zernike
  and Shack–Hartmann helpers, an air index with temperature and pressure, an ice material, silicon
  absorption, phone sensor formats in `O.cam.SENSORS`, a defocus blur-disc helper, a Ritchey–Chrétien
  design, numeric loss fields in `O.fibre.TYPES`.
- The full `simtest.js HYPER-OPTICS` in one process takes more than ten minutes and about 500 MB; run it
  per topic (`--only <topic>`) or in the background with a long limit.
